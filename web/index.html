How it works

Draw. Check. Fly.

1 · Draw

Search for your site and draw the area, or tap Map this view. The flight plan appears the moment you let go: height, overlap, speed and batteries all worked out.

2 · Check

Best flying windows for the week, wind, geomagnetic storms, airspace ceilings, tall obstacles and the ground under every line, all in plain words.

3 · Fly

Install straight onto a DJI RC 2 or your phone, or use a microSD package for the DJI RC. Every split mission gets its own battery and launch point.

Built for the field

Everything a mapping flight needs. Nothing it doesn't.

Follows the ground

Keeps the same height above hills and valleys using Kentucky LiDAR, USGS 3DEP or worldwide elevation data, with extra points over ridges.

Warns about trees and roofs

In Kentucky, GridFly checks the whole route against the tops of trees and buildings and tells you before the drone gets close.

Splits by signal range

Big areas are cut into zones, each with its own launch point, so the drone never flies farther from you than your signal reaches.

Checks the site for you

A 7-day forecast with the best windows, Kp index, FAA airspace and LAANC guidance, and nearby towers and masts from OpenStreetMap.

Works with no signal

Install it as an app, save the maps, ground heights and forecast before you go, and plan or adjust missions anywhere.

Sharp photos automatically

Picks the fastest speed that keeps blur under half a pixel, never spins at line ends, and checks every photo after the flight.

New · From field to finished map

Drop the photos in when you land. GridFly finds blurry shots and gaps, builds a mission that re-flies only the gaps, stitches a quick map in your browser, and hands everything to OpenDroneMap for a full-quality orthomosaic or 3D model.

Questions

Straight answers.

Is it really free?

Yes. GridFly is open-source software under the MIT licence. There is no paid tier, no trial and no account.

How does the mission get onto my drone?

DJI Fly can't import files, so GridFly replaces a placeholder mission you saved in DJI Fly. With a DJI RC 2, RC Pro or an Android phone, a small Windows helper does it over USB and keeps a backup. For the standard DJI RC you get a microSD package, and on iPhone you copy the file with the Files app.

Do my photos get uploaded anywhere?

No. The photo check and the quick map run entirely in your browser. Photos only leave your device if you choose to send them to an OpenDroneMap server yourself.

Can I use it with no signal?

Yes. Install GridFly as an app, then save your site before you go: satellite maps, ground heights and the site check are kept on your device.

Does it make the actual map?

It makes a quick map in your browser, good for checking coverage on site and for a first look. For a survey-grade orthomosaic or 3D model it prepares everything for OpenDroneMap (free), and can send the photos to a NodeODM server for you.

Is it safe?

GridFly checks heights, terrain, airspace and obstacles, but you are the pilot. Always check the mission on the controller before you fly, keep the drone in sight and follow your local rules. GridFly is not affiliated with DJI.

    Turn the photos into a map

    A quick map is stitched right here in your browser from the photo positions, good for checking the job on site. For a survey-grade map or 3D model, send the photos to OpenDroneMap.

    Full-quality map with NodeODM (experimental)

    NodeODM is OpenDroneMap's free processing server. Run it on your computer (docker run -p 3000:3000 opendronemap/nodeodm) or use one you have access to, then send the photos straight from here.
